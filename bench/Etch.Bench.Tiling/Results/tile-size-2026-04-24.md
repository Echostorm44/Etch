# Tile Size Benchmark Results

**Date**: 2026-04-24
**Machine**: Windows 10.0.26200 (x64)
**Runtime**: .NET 10.0.5, RyuJIT AVX-512

## Methodology

BenchmarkDotNet ShortRun: 3 iterations × 3 warmup iterations.
Scene: TextHeavy (5000 glyph-sized FillRect commands, 1920×1080 viewport).
Benchmark: `TileSizeBenchmark.ClassifyAndEmit()` — BBoxClassifier + StripEmitter pipeline.

## Results — TextHeavy Scene

| TileSize | Mean       | StdDev    | Op/s    | Allocated |
|----------|------------:|----------:|--------:|----------:|
| 8×8     | 224.771 ms | 5.197 ms |   4.4 K |   6.54 MB |
| 16×16   |  29.579 ms | 0.547 ms |  33.8 K |   3.88 MB |
| 32×32   |   5.929 ms | 0.053 ms | 168.7 K |   3.21 MB |

## Analysis

32×32 tiles are **5× faster** than 16×16 and **38× faster** than 8×8 for this scene.

Larger tiles reduce:
- Classification overhead (fewer tiles to iterate)
- Strip emission overhead (fewer tile-boundary crossings)
- Memory allocations (fewer Strips/Coverage byte[] allocations)

## Notes

- UiChrome and VectorArt scenes had execution issues (antivirus interference) during this run.
- The 32×32 advantage is expected to hold across all scene types due to reduced tile count.
- DefaultTileSize updated to 32.
